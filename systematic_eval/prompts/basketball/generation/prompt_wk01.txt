Optimization focus
- Only apply shortcuts that are realistic for this basketball data and likely to speed up queries. You may not drop rare/edge cases to simplify the plan.
- Do not invent tables/columns. Use exactly the column names from the schema (they are case-sensitive and used quoted).
- Prefer sargable predicates (e.g. year bounds) that the optimizer can use to prune scans.

Basketball-specific, world-knowledge-driven shortcuts you should consider when applicable
- League narrowing: if the query already implies a particular league, the same league usually holds on a joined table; consider adding it there too.
- Era / year sanity: Think about which time period the query's intention addresses. A league, conference or competition often implies a year range you can add as a bound to reduce scans.
- Three-point activity: if the query addresses non-zero three-point stats, which seasons can it realistically cover?
- Conference scope: which era does a conference filter imply? Use it to narrow the years if helpful.
- Postseason: if the query joins postseason tables or uses Post* columns, take into account what reaching the playoffs implies that you could also filter on.
- Well-known franchises/players: When filtering for well-known teams or players that you know, take into account your knowledge about when and where they existed (league, conference, era) to prune tables with filters.
- Think of more and other filters that can be applied, depending on the query's tables and filters.
